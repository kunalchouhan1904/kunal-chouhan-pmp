Six Principles • Three Mindsets

How to think like a PM

🧭 Proactive mindset

  • Adopt a holistic view: consider the project, organization, environment and society.
  • Embed quality: build quality into processes and deliverables, not just final inspection.

🤝 Ownership mindset

  • Be an accountable leader: act with integrity and transparency; own decisions.
  • Build an empowered culture: encourage trust, psychological safety, collaboration and learning.

🎯 Value-driven mindset

  • Focus on value: align decisions to business objectives and the value proposition.
  • Integrate sustainability: consider environmental, social and economic outcomes.

Risk, Conflict & Procurement

Choose the response that fits

Risk responses

  • Avoid: eliminate the threat.
  • Transfer: shift ownership/impact to a third party.
  • Mitigate: reduce probability or impact.
  • Accept: acknowledge the risk.
  • Escalate: move it to the appropriate level when outside project authority.
  • Opportunities: exploit, share, enhance, accept or escalate.

Conflict resolution

Order shown in the reference, from collaborative to least preferred:

  1. Collaborate / problem solve
  2. Compromise / reconcile
  3. Smooth / accommodate
  4. Force / direct
  5. Withdraw / avoid

Contract risk trend

Approximate seller → buyer risk progression:

FFP→FPIF→CPIF→CPAF→CPFF→T&M

Fixed-price generally places more cost risk on the seller; time-and-materials generally places more on the buyer.

Agile, Hybrid & Team Development

Tailor to context

Scrum

  • Product Owner: prioritises the product backlog.
  • Scrum Master: servant leader who helps remove impediments.
  • Developers: self-managing delivery team.
  • Events: Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Retrospective.
  • Artifacts: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment.

Methods & measures

  • Kanban: visual workflow, WIP limits, pull-based flow.
  • Lean: eliminate waste and improve flow.
  • SAFe: enterprise scaling; ART and PI Planning.
  • Hybrid: combine predictive and adaptive approaches.
  • Empirical: transparency → inspection → adaptation.
  • Velocity: planning aid, not a team performance KPI.
  • MoSCoW: Must, Should, Could, Won't.

Team growth & charts

Tuckman: Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.

Charts: burndown, burnup and cumulative flow diagram (CFD).

Use feedback loops, visible work, retrospectives and continuous improvement.

Leadership, Motivation & Stakeholders

People domain essentials

Leadership styles

  • Servant: support the team and remove blockers.
  • Transformational: inspire vision and change.
  • Transactional: clear rewards and consequences.
  • Situational: adapt to team needs and maturity.
  • Laissez-faire: hands-off delegation when appropriate.

Motivation theories

  • Maslow: hierarchy of needs.
  • Herzberg: hygiene factors and motivators.
  • McGregor: Theory X and Theory Y.
  • McClelland: achievement, affiliation and power.

Power / interest grid

  • High power + high interest: manage closely.
  • High power + low interest: keep satisfied.
  • Low power + high interest: keep informed.
  • Low power + low interest: monitor.

Engagement levels

Unaware → Resistant → Neutral → Supportive → Leading.

Assess current versus desired engagement and plan actions to close the gap.

Change Control & Organizational Change

Do not skip governance

Formal change control

Identify change→Submit request→Assess impact→CCB review→Approve / reject→Update documents and communicate

Evaluate impact and follow the agreed change-control process before implementing a baseline change.

Change models

ADKAR: Awareness → Desire → Knowledge → Ability → Reinforcement.

Kotter: Urgency → Coalition → Vision → Communicate → Empower → Quick wins → Consolidate → Anchor.

AI in Project Management

Use responsibly

Potential use cases

  • Schedule optimization and resource allocation.
  • Risk identification and probability analysis.
  • Stakeholder sentiment and communication analysis.
  • Cost estimation, forecasting and defect detection.

Responsible AI principles

  • Be transparent about AI use.
  • Check for bias and protect data privacy.
  • Keep human oversight and validate outputs.
  • The PM remains accountable for AI-assisted decisions.

Question Types & Exam-Day Strategy

Apply the scenario

Question formats listed in the attached reference

  • Case studies: linked questions; read the scenario first.
  • Enhanced matching: match items to categories (computer-based testing).
  • Graphic-based: interpret charts and diagrams.
  • Multiple response: select multiple correct answers.
  • Point and click: identify a hotspot (computer-based testing).
  • Pull-down: select from a dropdown in a diagram or table.
  • Traditional MCQ: eliminate clearly incorrect options first.

Exam-day checklist

  • Use the PMI mindset and choose the best answer for the given context.
  • Read keywords carefully: FIRST, NEXT, BEST, MOST, LEAST.
  • Budget roughly 80 seconds per question; mark difficult items and return if time permits.
  • Give Business Environment special attention because it is 26% in this reference.
  • Choose predictive, Agile or hybrid based on the scenario.
  • For case studies, read the scenario first; context may carry across linked questions.
  • Ignore sunk costs when deciding future actions; focus on future value.
